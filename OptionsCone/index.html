What you see

Option chain heatmaps on the price chart

The cone

Every dot is a listed contract. The color behind it is the probability that the price touches that strike before that expiry: dark near the money, fading to pale where a touch becomes unlikely. The cone widens with time because the price has longer to travel.

Three models

Implied: from the option prices, with each contract's own volatility.
10y: industry-standard probability models built on the stock's realized moves of the last ten years. Pro
Adaptive: builds on these models and adds what history misses: today's conditions, the events ahead and how the stock typically responds to them. Pro

Both realized models are tested against what actually happened (see the results).

Contour lines

Pick a probability and each model draws a line through the listed strikes that match it at every expiry, above and below spot. Where the lines separate, the market and the stock's realized moves disagree.

  1. Candle readout. Open, high, low and close of the day under the pointer.
  2. Price chart tools. Two moving averages, trend and horizontal lines, daily or weekly candles, ten years of history.
  3. Contour lines. The strikes that match your chosen probability at every expiry, one line per model.
  4. Expiry badges. The implied volatility of each expiry, and how it compares with the stock's realized volatility.
  5. Selected contract. Click a dot and its tooltip stays open: delta, gamma, implied volatility, touch and expire probabilities from the option market and, with Pro, from the realized odds.
  6. Earnings dates. Filled where confirmed, hollow where projected; an expiry that spans a report prices a different risk.

What it is for

Not only for option traders

Option prices contain the market's view of how likely future stock prices are. OptionsCone turns that view, and the stock's own history, into plain probabilities you can use whether or not you ever trade an option.

Build realistic scenarios

  • How far can the stock plausibly move by a given date, up and down?
  • What are the chances my price target or my break-even is reached?
  • How much does the market price around the next earnings date?

Manage risk at entry

  • How likely is a stop level to be hit during my holding period?
  • Where are large positions sitting, and are the quotes tight enough to trade?
  • Is there an earnings date inside my horizon?

Identify opportunities Pro

  • Where does the market price in a higher probability than the stock realized?
  • What is a short option worth at expiration, measured against its premium?
  • How much can a short option cost me, on average and in the bad cases, for the premium I collect?
  • Where could dealer hedging slow down or speed up a move?

The approach

Every short option carries risk.
It deserves due diligence.

Most tools show you the numbers. OptionsCone puts them in context, contract by contract: what the market prices, what the stock itself supports, and what selling is worth.

Each download pulls the full option chain and the stock's price history, checks every quote and solves each contract's volatility with a model for American options that accounts for dividends. Impossible quotes are removed and doubtful ones flagged, and a data quality report shows how many passed. Nothing is hidden behind a score.

For a single download of SPY, that means more than 3,000 contracts and ten years of price history, around 37,000 quality checks and more than 118,000 figures behind the cone. That takes a moment, and it should.

OptionsCone is made for decisions that take more than a glance: which option to sell, at which strike and expiry, and whether the premium is worth the risk.

On every test date, both models predicted the chances for the same strikes: calls and puts at four distances from the price, for terms from two weeks to six months. That makes more than two million predictions for the stocks alone, each one compared with what the stock actually did. The textbook formula is the random walk at today's volatility, without an expected return, the same kind of formula many brokers use for their probability figures.

Limitations. For gold and silver the miss is larger than for stocks, though still below the textbook formula, mainly because calls came out too low. ETF puts from two to six months were rated slightly too high. The stocks are today's S&P 500 members, which rose more after each test date than the average stock at the time. That favors a model that expects some rise over the textbook formula, which expects none. The ETFs have no such bias and show the same picture. The test covers 2016 to 2026, with the 2020 crash and the 2022 bear market, but no crisis like 2008. All misses are raw values and include the random noise of any finite sample.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is the probability of touch?

The probability that the stock price reaches a given strike at any time before expiry, even if it moves away again afterwards. It is always at least as high as the probability of finishing beyond the strike, and often about twice as high.

Do I need to trade options to use it?

No. The option market is only the source of one of the three views. If you hold stocks, the same chart tells you how likely a stop level, a price target or your break-even is to be reached within a given time, by the market's pricing and by the stock's own history. See the use cases.

What is the difference between the implied and the realized odds?

The implied odds come from option prices, so they reflect what the market charges for the risk today. The realized odds come from the stock's own moves. 10y uses industry-standard probability models built on the stock's realized moves of the last ten years. Adaptive builds on these models and adds what history misses: today's conditions, the events ahead and how the stock typically responds to them. This lets you compare the market's expectations with the realized moves of each individual stock. Where the two differ, the option market is pricing a move the history did not produce, or the other way round.

Can I see the numbers behind the colors?

Yes. The data table lists whatever the cone shows, strike by expiry, for the contracts that are actually listed. Switch it to delta to compare a whole chain with your broker at once. The Methods section explains what every number means.

Is OptionsCone free?

Yes. The probability cone and the implied volatility, open interest, spread and time value maps are free. Pro adds the realized odds, implied vs realized, the expected value and risk maps for sellers, and the gamma exposure map (GEX). See OptionsCone Pro for prices.

Why does a chart show older data in the evening?

Option prices are only meaningful while the US market is open. Outside trading hours, the app shows your most recent download from the trading session, with its date. If a ticker has never been downloaded during trading hours, the app tells you so: download it again between 9:30 and 16:00 New York time, usually 15:30 to 22:00 in Central Europe.

Does the app send my data anywhere?

No. Everything you download stays in a folder on your computer. The app only asks our server whether a newer version is out, and Pro checks its license there. Both send just the version, an anonymous computer ID and, for Pro, your key, never any of your data. The privacy notice has the details.

Why does Windows show a warning when I start it?

The executable is not code-signed, which costs money every year. SmartScreen warns about any unsigned program it has not seen often. Click More info, then Run anyway. Each release lists a SHA-256 checksum of the file, so you can check that it was not altered.

Does it run on Mac or Linux?

Not yet. OptionsCone is available for Windows 10 and 11.

Is this financial advice?

No. All probabilities are estimates from market prices or from the past. They are not forecasts, and the data can be delayed, incomplete or wrong.
