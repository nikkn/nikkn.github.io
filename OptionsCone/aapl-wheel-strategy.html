AAPL wheel strategy: how fast did assigned AAPL shares get back to the strike?

By Nikolai Alexander · 19 November 2026

The wheel on AAPL runs in three steps: sell a put, take the shares if you are assigned, then sell covered calls until the shares are called away. This guide shows how each step went for AAPL on every trading day of the last ten years, next to the average S&P 500 stock.

AAPL moves a little less than the average S&P 500 stock, about 0.9 times as much. Over 30 days, one typical move was about 7.3% at its usual volatility of 24% over the last ten years. It reports earnings four times a year, and on report day the stock moved a median 3.3%, against 3.2% for the average stock. In late October 2026 its volatility over the previous three months was about 19%, which puts one typical move at roughly 5.6%.

A typical move is the stock's volatility over the previous three months, scaled to 30 days. All strikes in this guide sit a number of typical moves from the price, so the distances follow AAPL's own swings instead of a fixed percentage.

Step 1: selling the put

The wheel starts with a sold put, usually a cash secured put. If AAPL stays above the strike, the put expires worthless and you keep the premium. The table shows how often that happened for 30-day puts.

Measured in its own typical moves, AAPL's puts expired worthless about as often as the average stock's at every distance. In percent, the strikes sat a little closer to the price, because AAPL swings a little less.

Earnings: unlike for most stocks, a report inside the 30 days did not lower the share for AAPL's puts. One typical move below the price, 88% of AAPL's puts expired worthless with a report and 86% without. For the average stock it was 85% and 88%.

Touched before expiry: a bit more than one put in four at one typical move was touched before expiry (28%). Of those, 52% still expired worthless, against 54% for the average stock, so a touch alone did not decide the trade.

Step 2: if you get assigned

When a put one typical move below the price finished in the money, AAPL closed a median 3.0% below the strike at expiry, and in one case in ten more than 10% below it. For the average stock it was 3.5% and 13%.

The question for the wheel is how long the shares take to get back to the strike, the price you paid for them. The table shows how often AAPL closed at or above the strike at least once within 30, 60 and 90 days after the assignment.

Assigned AAPL shares got back to the strike clearly more often than those of the average stock, and they also fell less far below it. About one put in eight finished in the money, so these numbers rest on about 330 assignments over ten years. That makes them less certain than the numbers for the average stock, but the gap of about 11 points held at all three horizons.

Step 3: the covered call

With the shares in the account, the wheel continues with covered calls. If AAPL stays below the strike, the call expires worthless, you keep the premium and the shares. The table shows how often that happened for 30-day calls.

AAPL's calls expired worthless less often than the average stock's at every distance, most clearly close to the price. Earnings made the biggest difference: one typical move above the price, 65% of the calls expired worthless when a report fell inside the 30 days, and 82% without one. For the average stock it was 77% and 85%.

When the shares were called away, AAPL closed a median 3.2% above the strike at expiry, and in one case in ten more than 8% above it. That gain goes to the buyer of the call. For the average stock it was 2.9% and 10%.

AAPL wheel FAQ

How often do AAPL puts expire worthless?

From November 2016 to October 2026, 87% of 30-day AAPL puts one typical move below the price expired worthless, about 7% below the price at AAPL's usual volatility. That is the same as for the average S&P 500 stock. Half a typical move below it was 77%, two typical moves below it 97%.

How long did AAPL take to get back to the strike after an assignment?

Of the puts one typical move below the price that finished in the money, AAPL closed at or above the strike again within 30 days in 70% of cases, within 60 days in 81% and within 90 days in 88%. For the average S&P 500 stock it was 58%, 70% and 77%.

Do covered calls on AAPL get called away often?

More often than on the average stock. One typical move above the price, 77% of AAPL's 30-day calls expired worthless, against 83% for the average S&P 500 stock, and only 65% when an earnings report fell inside the 30 days. When the shares were called away, AAPL closed a median 3.2% above the strike.
