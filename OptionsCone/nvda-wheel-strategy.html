NVDA wheel strategy: how often puts and calls expired worthless

By Nikolai Alexander · 16 November 2026

The wheel on NVDA runs in three steps: sell a put, take the shares if you are assigned, then sell covered calls until the shares are called away. This guide shows how each step went for NVDA on every trading day of the last ten years, next to the average S&P 500 stock.

NVDA moves about 1.7 times as much as the average S&P 500 stock. Over 30 days, one typical move was about 13.5% at its usual volatility of 44% over the last ten years. It reports earnings four times a year, and on report day the stock moved a median 5.2%, against 3.2% for the average stock. In late October 2026 its volatility over the previous three months was about 35%, which puts one typical move at roughly 11%.

A typical move is the stock's volatility over the previous three months, scaled to 30 days. All strikes in this guide sit a number of typical moves from the price, so the distances follow NVDA's own swings instead of a fixed percentage.

Step 1: selling the put

The wheel starts with a sold put, usually a cash secured put. If NVDA stays above the strike, the put expires worthless and you keep the premium. The table shows how often that happened for 30-day puts.

Measured in its own typical moves, NVDA's puts expired worthless more often than the average stock's, most clearly close to the price. In percent, the strikes simply sat further away.

Earnings: a report inside the 30 days lowered the share a little, about as much as for the average stock. One typical move below the price, 90% of NVDA's puts expired worthless with a report and 92% without. For the average stock it was 85% and 88%.

Touched before expiry: about one put in four at one typical move was touched before expiry (24%). Of those, 64% still expired worthless, against 54% for the average stock, so a touch alone did not decide the trade.

Step 2: if you get assigned

When a put one typical move below the price finished in the money, NVDA closed a median 5.4% below the strike at expiry, and in one case in ten more than 18% below it. For the average stock it was 3.5% and 13%.

The question for the wheel is how long the shares take to get back to the strike, the price you paid for them. The table shows how often NVDA closed at or above the strike at least once within 30, 60 and 90 days after the assignment.

Assigned NVDA shares got back about as often as those of the average stock, and about one in four was still below the strike 90 days later. Assignments were rare, though: only about one put in twelve finished in the money, so these NVDA numbers rest on few cases, and the small gaps to the average stock may be chance. When NVDA did fall through the strike, it fell further, which is where its larger swings show most.

Step 3: the covered call

With the shares in the account, the wheel continues with covered calls. If NVDA stays below the strike, the call expires worthless, you keep the premium and the shares. The table shows how often that happened for 30-day calls.

NVDA's calls expired worthless less often than the average stock's at every distance. The stock trended up strongly over these ten years, and earnings widened the gap: one typical move above the price, 74% of the calls expired worthless when a report fell inside the 30 days, and 82% without one. For the average stock it was 77% and 85%.

When the shares were called away, NVDA closed a median 5.8% above the strike at expiry, and in one case in ten more than 22% above it. That gain goes to the buyer of the call. For the average stock it was 2.9% and 10%.

Data and limits

NVDA daily closing, high and low prices for the last ten years, November 2016 to October 2026, adjusted for splits. Every trading day is a start date (2,493 starts). 30 days means 21 trading days. Worthless means the close on the last day was on the safe side of the strike. Touched means the daily high or low reached it. The average stock pools 469 of today's S&P 500 members over the same years.

  • NVDA was one of the best-performing stocks of these ten years, and the average stock is made of today's index members, so both look back on a history of winners. A strong uptrend favors puts and works against covered calls, and the coming years need not look the same.
  • The strikes are set with the stock's past volatility. Implied volatility is usually higher, so a strike one typical move away by the implied volatility your platform shows sits a little further out.
  • All numbers are shares of many past trades, not a prediction for a single trade. NVDA's numbers also come from one stock only and are less certain than those of the average stock.

NVDA wheel FAQ

How often do NVDA puts expire worthless?

From November 2016 to October 2026, 92% of 30-day NVDA puts one typical move below the price expired worthless, about 12% below the price at NVDA's usual volatility. Half a typical move below it was 81%, two typical moves below it 98%.

How long did NVDA take to get back to the strike after an assignment?

Of the puts one typical move below the price that finished in the money, NVDA closed at or above the strike again within 30 days in 56% of cases, within 60 days in 67% and within 90 days in 73%. The average S&P 500 stock got back about as often: 58%, 70% and 77%.

Do covered calls on NVDA get called away often?

More often than on the average stock. One typical move above the price, 79% of NVDA's 30-day calls expired worthless, against 83% for the average S&P 500 stock, and only 74% when an earnings report fell inside the 30 days. When the shares were called away, NVDA closed a median 5.8% above the strike.
